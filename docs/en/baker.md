# Baker and Arnold transformations

## What you see

A figure (a cat face, a disk or a cloud of points) in the unit square, our phase space. Each iteration applies an **area-preserving** map: the baker's stretches the figure twofold in $x$, squeezes it to half in $y$ and stacks the right half on the left; Arnold's cat deforms it linearly and wraps it on the torus. After a few iterations the figure is unrecognisable and the points cover the square uniformly: the plot on the right measures that uniformity. After many more, the computer's finite arithmetic makes the points collapse.

## Physical model

These are the simplest models of **ergodic and mixing** dynamics in a phase space. The baker's transformation,

$$x' = 2x \bmod 1,\qquad y' = \tfrac12\big(y + \lfloor 2x\rfloor\big),$$

and Arnold's toral automorphism,

$$\begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = T\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} \bmod 1,\qquad T = \begin{pmatrix} t_{11} & t_{12} \\ t_{21} & t_{22}\end{pmatrix},\quad \det T = 1,$$

have unit Jacobian (Liouville's theorem: phase-space volume is preserved) and, if the eigenvalues of $T$ are real and different from 1, one direction stretching by $\lambda_+ > 1$ and one contracting by $\lambda_- = 1/\lambda_+$. Repeated stretching and folding is the mechanism of **mixing**: any region ends up spread uniformly, and the Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy, $h_{KS} = \ln\lambda_+$ ($\ln 2$ for the baker, $\ln\tfrac{3+\sqrt5}{2} \approx 0.96$ for the classic cat), measures how fast information about the initial condition is lost. With integer elements and $\det T = 1$ the map is a bijection of the torus; other matrices (complex eigenvalues, e.g. $t_{11} = 0$) rotate without mixing.

The collapse after ~50 iterations is not physics: 53-bit floating-point numbers form a finite set and Arnold's cat, as an integer map, eventually sends every representable point to short periodic orbits and to the origin.

## Algorithm

Double-precision points; the cat face is generated by rejection inside a disk with ears, eyes and mouth. Uniformity: $\chi^2/{\rm dof}$ of the counts in a $10\times10$ grid against $n/100$. For the cat, the fourth element $t_{22} = (1 + t_{12}t_{21})/t_{11}$ is fixed by $\det T = 1$.

## Tests

`tests/fises1.test.js`: a uniform cloud stays uniform under the baker ($\chi^2/{\rm dof} < 1.6$); the completed Arnold matrix is $[[1,1],[1,2]]$ with $\lambda_+ = (3+\sqrt5)/2$; a disk of radius 0.3 ends with the fraction $\pi r^2$ of points inside its initial outline after 12 cat iterations; the baker maps $(1/4,1/2) \to (1/2,1/4)$ exactly.

## Didactic assessment

It visualises Liouville, ergodicity, mixing and sensitivity to initial conditions in seconds, plus a real example of a numerical artefact. Source: *Experimentos Interactivos* course (UCM), Statistical Physics experiment 3, R. Brito; J. R. Dorfman, *An Introduction to Chaos in Nonequilibrium Statistical Mechanics* (1999).
